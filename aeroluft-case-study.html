01 / Context

The complaint should not become a relay race.

The project was conceived around a simple operational problem: customer complaints need to move quickly from first contact to a useful resolution path, without making the customer become the technician or forcing the service team to reconstruct the case manually.

The goal was a system where the customer gets a simple guided experience, technicians receive a structured brief, and the internal team can see the entire ticket lifecycle in one place.

03 / Customer flow

Never ask the customer to think like an engineer.

The customer-facing journey was designed around one question at a time, with a target of roughly 4–7 interactions. The backend decision tree stays invisible.

Identify

Capture customer, location and equipment information required to work the case.

Check safety

Safety conditions can force technician involvement without automatically forcing a site visit.

Diagnose

Guide the customer through structured fault patterns and remote troubleshooting.

Resolve remotely

Level 1 paths attempt tool-guided or technician-guided resolution before dispatch.

Book when needed

Phone and site-visit flows use defined slots and calendar rules.

Confirm closure

Customer confirmation prevents premature closure for self-installation or unresolved cases.

04 / Engineering

Operational rules became product rules.

The system was designed around explicit business rules rather than a generic ticket form.

Rule 01

Remote-first routing

Every suitable path attempts remote troubleshooting before technician dispatch.

Rule 02

Three resolution levels

Level 1 app fix, Level 2 technician intervention, Level 3 Service Head escalation.

Rule 03

Safety is different

A safety issue forces technician involvement, but does not automatically mean a site visit.

Rule 04

Closure needs evidence

Customer self-installation tickets remain open until the customer confirms resolution.

Rule 05

AI stays bounded

Diagnosis recommendations are only surfaced when the fault pattern exists in the troubleshooting library.

Rule 06

Human escalation remains human

Service Head escalation triggers internal discussion rather than an automatic action.

05 / What was built

A working MVP, not just a flowchart.

The project material defines and builds a substantial MVP across the customer, technician and internal service layers.

Master wireframe

1,240-line specification covering screens, navigation, data model, error states and ticket lifecycle.

Customer web tool

16+ interactive screens spanning identification, safety, diagnosis, quick fixes, booking, resolution and feedback.

Service desk

Ticket queue, detail views, warranty handling and closure guard.

Apps Script backend

Ticket CRUD, WhatsApp greeting logic, Telegram alert logic and structured Sheets integration.

Booking logic

Real dates, phone/site slots, and rules excluding Sundays and 2nd/4th Saturdays.

Data structure

Tickets, Customers, Equipment, Technicians, Holidays, Leads, AuditLog and Config tabs.

06 / Outcome

The operating model became visible.

The intended outcome is not another dashboard. It is a service process where every complaint has an explicit next step, whether that is a remote fix, technician intervention, escalation or confirmed closure.

Core target: 100% of complaints receive a resolution or a clear resolution path within 24 hours.

The project also establishes measurable operating signals: first response time, remote resolution rate, customer confirmation rate, repeated unresolved rate, average questions per complaint and technician/phone resolution rate.

07 / Status & next

Built foundation. Production is the next layer.

The MVP/design work includes the customer tool, service desk, Apps Script logic, Google Sheets structure and booking logic. Production integrations remain separate work rather than being presented as completed.
